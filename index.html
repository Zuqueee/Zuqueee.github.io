GoHighLevel

Field notes from running GHL across dozens of sub-accounts. These are the failures that don't show up as errors — the ones that quietly cost you leads for months.

Inbound webhooks match by key name, and nothing warns you when they don't

A landing page that nests its quiz answers inside quiz or answers, or renames service to project, delivers a contact with every qualification field blank. The webhook returns 200. The workflow completes. Sales gets a lead with no context and blames the ad account.

Snapshots preserve pipeline and stage IDs. They do not preserve custom field IDs

This is the one that breaks cloned sub-accounts. Your workflow ports across fine and the stage triggers still fire, but every custom field reference points at an ID that doesn't exist in the new location. Rolling out a snapshot means re-collecting the field IDs per location, not copying and hoping.

Phone numbers must be E.164 before they reach GHL

A number stored as (305) 555-0147 is a dead string. It looks correct in the contact record and the dialer and SMS can't use it. Normalize to +13055550147 at the source and both work.

Contact name mapping varies per sub-account

Pages posting straight to GHL should send the name in both snake_case and camelCase — first_name and firstName — because the inbound mapping isn't identical across locations. I've watched contacts arrive nameless for weeks because one sub-account expected the other casing.

Pipeline stage changes are the cleanest conversion trigger you have

Stage-change automations firing a webhook are what let you send a real booked appointment back to Meta as a server-side event, instead of optimizing on form fills. The CRM already knows which leads are real — the job is getting that back to the platform inside the attribution window.

Know where GHL ends and n8n begins

GHL workflows are excellent at reacting to CRM state and poor at transforming data. Anything involving payload normalization, hashing, deduplication, external API calls or round-robin logic across sub-accounts belongs in n8n. Trying to force it into a GHL workflow is how accounts end up with forty brittle steps nobody dares touch.

Selected work
GTF Construction LLC Metal buildings & kitchens · Palm Beach / Martin, FL Pay-per-appointment

Two flights, 153 landing page views, zero leads. The budget was on the wrong product.

Context

A top-tier account billed per booked appointment — 15 a month at a $150 target cost. Two product lines ran out of one Meta ad account and one GoHighLevel sub-account: kitchen remodeling and metal buildings. The kitchen campaign had burned $713 across two flights and produced 153 landing page views and not one lead. It had already been paused once because the dashboard read zero.

Diagnosis

The reflex read is broken tracking, and I wrote that conclusion first — then checked it against the n8n execution log and the Meta Marketing API instead of trusting it. It was wrong. Same pixel, same n8n workflow, same GHL location, the metal-building line had taken 92 leads from 628 views. The event worked. Three things were killing the kitchen line: 39% of its traffic was Audience Network (an_classic served 5,532 impressions for $10.31, and rewarded_video is a click for a prize), the audience was ten ZIP codes with a reach of 2,045 at frequency 4.02 in six days with the largest budget slice on women 65+, and the ad set was optimizing toward a custom conversion that had never fired once — the model had no converter to find lookalikes of. P(0 leads in 153 views | 5% conversion) = 0.04%. On the same account in the same period, the other line converted at 16%.

Intervention

I stopped defending the line that didn't convert and consolidated the budget into the one that did. Audience Network off, geography opened, age band rebuilt, and the optimization event moved off the zero-history custom conversion onto the event with 92 real conversions behind it. I also found the landing page never sent page_url, so the n8n node fell back to the referer header — and in 10 of 90 executions there was no referer at all, meaning ~11% of events reached Meta with no source URL and could never satisfy the conversion's own URL rule. One line of code.

Result

Week of Oct 26 – Nov 1: $351.49 of spend, 26 leads at $13.52, and 16 booked appointments — up from 11 the week before. The contract asked for 15 a month; that week alone delivered 16, at roughly $22 per booked appointment against a $150 target. The appointments booked on this account have closed roughly $700,000 in signed work.

Correia Construction Inc Siding · Fall River, MA 18-appointment package

Three leads vanished between Meta and the CRM, and nothing logged an error.

Context

A siding contractor in Fall River, Massachusetts. New ad account, new landing page, a contracted package of 18 booked appointments by December 1 — and no offer. The client arrived without one, in a market where the offer is the whole game.

Diagnosis

Two days after launch Meta reported 3 Lead events and the CRM had none. Neither n8n nor GoHighLevel had ever seen them. The landing page was submitting through navigator.sendBeacon with a JSON body — which CORS rejects as a non-simple request, silently. No console error, no failed request in the network tab, nothing in any log, and no way to recover the three people who had filled out the form. Separately, the GHL inbound workflow was still sitting in draft, which would have swallowed the next batch too.

Intervention

I moved the submit to a plain fetch against n8n only, and made the pixel's Lead fire after the 200 instead of before it — so the dashboard can no longer report a lead the CRM doesn't have. Then I built the offer the account was missing: I pulled 51 siding ads across MA and RI from 33 advertisers and found a market saturated with discount and “eligibility” angles recycled out of lead networks. Territory data split the campaign in two — Fall River and New Bedford are 25–32% detached homes at $54–56k income, while the surrounding ring holds 59,460 detached homes at $92–145k. What shipped was a price floor, not a monthly payment (“new siding starting at $5,000” plus gutters), which keeps the campaign out of Meta's special ad category and keeps ZIP-level targeting.

Result

At the mid-September reading the account stood at 1 of 18 appointments with 70 days left — the booking pace had to multiply 7.3× to make the contract. October 11: 13 of 18. November 1: 20 of 18 — the package closed a month before the contract date, with the last two booked on $89.58 of spend across three days of delivery. Those visits have closed $200,000 in signed siding work.

U.S. home improvement GoHighLevel + n8n + Meta CAPI ~20 landing pages

The leads were arriving. The qualification wasn't.

Context

A portfolio of contractor landing pages feeding GoHighLevel sub-accounts. Volume looked healthy, cost per lead looked fine, and sales kept saying the leads were garbage.

Diagnosis

I audited the webhook executions instead of the ad account. GHL matches inbound payloads by key name — and several pages were sending the qualification answers nested, or under aliases the CRM had never seen (project instead of service, timeline instead of timing). Those contacts landed with zero qualification fields. Worse: the conversion event fired on submit regardless, so Meta was being trained to find people who fill out forms, not people who buy.

Intervention

Patching each page meant waiting on deploys I didn't control. So I moved the fix upstream and wrote one normalizer at the ingestion layer in n8n: unnests payloads, resolves field aliases, splits names across every casing GHL expects, normalizes phone to E.164, and injects IP, user agent and source URL to lift Event Match Quality. Tested against seven real payload shapes — 7/7 recovered the full qualification set. Retroactive, and no deploy required.

Result

On the client funnel I measured closely: match quality went from 6.1 to 9.0, and the rebuilt funnel produced 42 booked appointments from 90 leads in 30 days — a 46.7% lead-to-appointment rate on leads that now arrive fully qualified.

Optimizing toward a booked appointment, not a form fill.

Context

Home-remodeling contractors paid per qualified appointment. The close can land four months after the click, which puts the sale far outside the attribution window the algorithm actually learns from.

Diagnosis

Two bad options were on the table. Optimizing on the purchase starves the campaign, because almost nothing gets attributed inside the window. Optimizing on the raw lead is cheap and dumb, because it counts everyone who touched the form. The right target sat in the middle, inside the CRM — and nobody was sending it back to the platform.

Intervention

I mapped progressive server-side events to GHL pipeline stages, and made the moment the appointment is booked the optimization target — it fits comfortably inside the 7-day click window. To avoid dropping the ad set into learning limited, I ran the new event in parallel with the old one until it cleared 50 conversions per week for two consecutive weeks, then switched the optimization and retired the proxy. The purchase event stayed live for ROAS measurement only, never for optimization.
